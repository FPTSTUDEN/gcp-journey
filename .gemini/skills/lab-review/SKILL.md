# Review a lab record

1. Read `TAXONOMY.md`, `GEMINI.md`, and the target lab record.
2. Inspect relevant available artifacts—terminal history, command output, logs, manifests, screenshots, test results, timestamps, and lab notes—within the permitted workspace. Do not access unrelated files, credentials, tokens, private keys, or sensitive project data.
3. Separate evidence provenance: `observed` means directly inspected, `reported` means the user stated it, `inferred` means AI analysis, and `unverified` means not sufficiently supported.
4. If the available evidence cannot establish how much work the learner did, ask one focused question about the specific step or result that matters. Do not ask the learner to classify tags manually.
5. Conclude progress explicitly as not-started, in-progress, partial, completed, or not-verified. Completion claims require supporting evidence; a lab description or course completion indicator alone is insufficient.
6. Check whether tags communicate useful distinctions across topic, skill, service, and context. Do not insist that every group be populated.
7. Be ambitious in analysis: identify meaningful concepts, relationships, likely learning value, and useful tag distinctions from the material.
8. Recommend per-tag evidence ranks from 1–3. Do not assign applied or integrated ranks solely from instructions; base them on observed or reported work and label the basis.
9. Make assumptions, recommendations, contradictions, and remaining gaps visible. The user owns final interpretations and can accept, edit, or reject them.
10. Do not infer that the learner personally performed a task just because the lab describes it.
